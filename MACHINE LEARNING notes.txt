MACHINE LEARNING: Attrition Prediction

-Target Variable = attrition
Data type = Boolean
False = still at the company
True = Left the company

This is a binary classification problem

-Business Objective
Identify employees/groups with elevated attrition risk
Prioritize potential retention interventions
Do not treat predictions as guaranteed outcomes. Standard errors still apply

This model will be used to predict the attrition probability of a given employee and classify them in 3 risk categories: 'Low', 'Medium', or 'High'. 


- Data Leakage Prevention
Divided datasets into various categories and inserted the relevant columns into their respective categories. I did this so that data I would have known after an employee leaves would be omitted
I have also omitted employee identifiers and verified if engineered variables have data leakage. 

Variables excluded are:
daily_rate, employee_number, hourly_rate, peformance_rating, total_working_years, years_since_last_promotion.

- Business Implication
False Negatives (predicting an employee will stay when they actually leave) are the more operationally dangerous error for this business. A false negative gives HR false confidence that a team is stable, so no retention intervention or backfill hiring is triggered. When that employee then leaves unexpectedly, the business is caught understaffed — facing lost productivity, rushed and often lower-quality replacement hiring, and the loss of institutional knowledge and team continuity that a planned transition would have preserved.

False Positives (predicting an employee will leave, when they actually stay) carry a different, generally less severe cost. Acting on a false positive means HR invests in retention efforts (or begins backfill hiring) for someone who was never actually at risk. In the worst case, this leads to overstaffing and unnecessary hiring spend — but this is typically a recoverable, budget-level cost, whereas the understaffing triggered by a false negative can disrupt operations and client/team continuity immediately and unpredictably.

- Conclusion
Because false negatives are costlier to this business than false positives, the model selection priority should favour minimizing false negatives (i.e., maximizing recall) even at some cost to precision. On that basis, Logistic Regression is the strongest candidate of the three
